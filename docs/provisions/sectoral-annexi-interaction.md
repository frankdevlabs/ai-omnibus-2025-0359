# Interaction with sectoral product legislation (Annex I)

**The trilogue-breaker** — the 28 Apr 2026 political trilogue collapsed after ~12 hours over
conformity assessment for AI embedded in Annex I products. Operative text:
[Commission](../../extracts/commission/COM-2025-836_sectoral-annexi-interaction.md) ·
[agreed](../../extracts/agreed/PE-789081_sectoral-annexi-interaction.md)

The agreed solution:

- **Machinery products** (Machinery Regulation): the **double conformity assessment is removed** —
  these products fall under sectoral safety rules instead of both AI Act + sectoral rules, under an
  **equivalence clause** (equivalent level of health/safety protection). The Commission may add
  health/safety requirements for high-risk-classified AI via **delegated acts** under the Machinery
  Regulation.
- **Other sectors** (medical devices, toys, lifts, radio equipment, watercraft): the Commission may
  limit the application of AI Act requirements via **implementing acts** where sectoral law contains
  comparable AI-specific requirements.
- New Commission duty to provide **operator guidance**.
- The **"safety component" definition is narrowed**: AI that merely supports users or optimises
  performance is outside high-risk unless its failure endangers health/safety (see
  [high-risk classification](high-risk-classification-and-dates.md)).

**What did not happen:** the EP wanted to go further — deleting Annex I Section A — but BEUC, CDT
and allies warned that would place consumer products (toys, medical devices) outside the AI Act
entirely ([stakeholders](../stakeholders.md)); the push did not fully succeed.

**Watch:** the equivalence clause's final wording is one of the flagged legal-linguistic revision
points (`STATUS.md`). The delegated/implementing-act empowerments create a post-adoption
comitology track worth tracking. Aviation runs on its own track:
[aviation-reg-2018-1139](aviation-reg-2018-1139.md).
